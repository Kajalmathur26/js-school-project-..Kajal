1822 – Mechanical Era: Babbage’s Difference Engine

Date: 1822 (partial portion built by 1832)

Charles Babbage designed one of the first mechanical calculators to solve polynomial equations using a method called “differences.” This laid the groundwork for the idea of computing machines.

1946 – Electronic Era: ENIAC

Date: Completed in 1945, unveiled in 1946

ENIAC was the first general-purpose, programmable electronic computer. It used around 17,000 vacuum tubes and could perform 5,000 calculations per second—a breakthrough at the time.

1951 – Commercial Era: UNIVAC I

Date: Launched June 14, 1951

UNIVAC I was the first computer built for business use in the U.S. Famously, it predicted the 1952 U.S. election results on live TV—demonstrating the power of computing to the public.

1971 – Microprocessor Revolution: Intel 4004

Date: Released in 1971

The Intel 4004 was the world’s first microprocessor on a single chip. This innovation made computers smaller, more affordable, and opened the door to personal computing.

1981 – PC Era: IBM Personal Computer (IBM 5150)

Date: Released in 1981

IBM’s personal computer became the gold standard for desktops. It made computing more accessible and brought PCs into everyday homes and workplaces.

2007 – Present: Mobile & AI Era

Date: Starting 2007

The launch of the iPhone sparked the mobile computing revolution. Since then, technologies like cloud computing, artificial intelligence, and even quantum computing have pushed the boundaries further.
